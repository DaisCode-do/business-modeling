# Business Lifecycles

The following lifecycles are proposed starting models. They define meaningful business stages, not interface labels.
Transition authority and exact policy must be confirmed through workflow review.

## Candidate lifecycles

The first workflow reviews should determine whether the business needs distinct
lifecycles for:

- project opportunity, authorization, active delivery, suspension, completion,
  closeout, cancellation, and warranty/follow-up;
- contract proposal, acceptance, effectiveness, change, completion,
  termination, and supersession;
- budget preparation, review, approval, supersession, and closure;
- purchase request, order/commitment, receipt, invoice review, obligation,
  settlement, dispute, void, and correction;
- contractor assignment, measured work, claim review, approval, rejection,
  settlement, and correction;
- client progress certification, invoice, collection, adjustment, cancellation,
  and reversal; and
- accounting/tax preparation, review, posting/submission, closing, correction,
  and reversal.

Do not create one universal `status` vocabulary. Each lifecycle should name its
own subject, permitted transitions, decision authority, evidence, effective
time, and historical correction behavior.

## Standard lifecycle review

For each concept, document:

1. what establishes it;
2. its meaningful business stages;
3. who may cause each transition;
4. prerequisites and required evidence;
5. normal and exceptional exits;
6. whether a stage is recorded or derived; and
7. how mistakes are corrected without erasing accepted history.
