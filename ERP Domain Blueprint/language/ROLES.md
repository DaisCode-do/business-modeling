# Business Roles

Business roles describe participation and responsibility. They do not directly define software permissions, employment
classification, or organizational rank. A person may hold several roles simultaneously or at different times.

## Candidate business roles

The client should confirm the names, responsibilities, and authority of these
roles through real workflows:

- **Client or client representative** — requests, accepts, approves, pays for,
  or receives information about project work.
- **Owner/administrator** — establishes company policy and authorizes selected
  commercial, financial, operational, and access decisions.
- **Project lead or manager** — coordinates scope, site work, resources,
  progress, exceptions, and project decisions.
- **Estimator/planner** — develops estimates, budgets, APU, schedules, forecasts,
  or proposed changes.
- **Purchasing participant** — requests, sources, orders, receives, or verifies
  goods and services.
- **Supplier** — provides goods or services and may issue an invoice.
- **Contractor** — undertakes defined work and may submit measured work or a
  claim under agreed terms.
- **Worker or crew member** — performs operational work under an employment,
  casual, contractor-crew, or other relationship that must be made explicit.
- **Accountant** — reviews or maintains financial and tax information and
  produces formal outputs within an agreed responsibility boundary.
- **Payment controller or approver** — authorizes, executes, or verifies money
  movement.
- **System user** — receives software access. This is an access relationship, not
  a synonym for administrator, employee, or any business role.

## Questions to record per workflow

- Who performs the step?
- Who owns the resulting record?
- Who may decide or approve an exception?
- Who must be informed?
- Who may view sensitive evidence?
- Can the same person hold conflicting responsibilities, and what control is
  required when they do?
